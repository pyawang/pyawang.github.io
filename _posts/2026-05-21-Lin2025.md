## Paper Review: [Evolution of Massive Red Galaxies in Clusters from z = 1.0 to z = 0.3]

### Quick Reference
* **arXiv Link:** [AJ/169.285](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/link_gateway/2025AJ....169..285L/doi:10.3847/1538-3881/adc0f8)
* **Key Authors:** [Y.T. Lin et al.]
* **Primary Field:** [Galaxy Clusters, Galaxy Evolution]

---

### The 1-Minute Pitch (TL;DR)
>  How do galaxy cluster evolve through time? The authors developed a method to connect observed galaxy clusters to their descendents through a cosmological simulation. The result shows the assembly of red galaxies, and the stellar mass are a monotonically growth through time, while the Brightest Central Galaxy (BCG) presents a decay in late time of all mass range.

---

### 1. Scientific Motivation & Context
* **The Knowledge Gap:** How do we connect observed galaxy clusters from high-z to low-z in order to find out the evolution? How do we know their relationship to their host dark matter halo? "Top N" selection method is one example that rank the galaxy clusters with their mass and assign progenitor-descendant relation to groups. However this ignored their scattering of observables while dark matter halo remains unable to estimate. 
* **Why it Matters:** Various method of connecting halo to cluster through their evolution should be develop to better improve the precision of these studies.

### 2. Methodology & Analytical Framework
* **Data & Tooling:** Galaxy cluster sample observed by HSC and selected through CAMIRA; merger trees are extracted from Millennium Run simulation which provide halo information. The author find the galaxy richness-mass relation from observed data in different redshift bins, and apply this relation to merger tree sample at certain redshift, finding the cluster with closest mass and connect different redshift with merger tree to group progenitor-descendents.
* **Core Approximations:** Redshift is limited to $z=[0.3,1]$.

### 3. Key Findings 
* **Major Results:** 
  1. Redshift evolution of total stellar mass showed a general enhance for all mass groups.
  2. Brighest Central Galaxy(BCG) mass are evolved quickly and followed by a sudden suppress for all mass groups.
  3. BCG mass to total stellar mass ratio decrease through time for all mass groups.
* **The Central Figure:** Figure 4, 5, 6 illustrated the three points above.

### 4. Critical Commentary & Future Horizon
* **Strengths:** They presented new method to connect galaxy clusters.
* **Limitations:** What are the connected "mass" mentioned in the method? Is it based on halo mass or stellar mass? If stellar mass, then the result might be a cycle that it is natural to have a monotonically growth through time.
