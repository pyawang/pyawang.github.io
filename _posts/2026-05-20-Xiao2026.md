## Paper Review: [Correlation between baryonic process and galaxy assembly bias]

### Quick Reference
* **arXiv Link:** [abs/2605.19323](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.19323)
* **Key Authors:** [Zilan Xiao et al.]
* **Primary Field:** [Galaxy Evolution, Galaxy Assembly Bias, Baryonic Process, semi-analytic model]

---

### The 1-Minute Pitch (TL;DR)
>  SAM selected, cosmological simulation created, galaxies are measured their clustering properties by two point correlation function. By seperate into top 20% SFR and top 20% stellar mass sample, they found the underlying baryonic process are different: stellar mass group is dominant by gas cooling and stellar feedback, while SFR group is star formation to gas cooling as a function of number density.

---

### 1. Scientific Motivation & Context
* **The Knowledge Gap:** What are the dependencies related to baryonic process on galaxy clustering other than halo mass (Galaxy assembly bias)? What can semianalytical model (SAM) tell us?
* **Why it Matters:** Observational results remain debated, making SAM result worth reference.

### 2. Methodology & Analytical Framework
* **Data & Tooling:** Use SAM to construct a galaxy catalogue from UNIT cosmological simulation (product from Z. Zhai et al. 2025) --> measure clustering signal by two point correlation function --> galaxy sample are shuffled (several times) after they are grouped in order to break large scale correlation signal
* **Core Approximations:** Only focus galaxy sample at $z=0.55$

### 3. Key Findings 
* **Major Results:** 
  1. The halo occupation varies significantly due the choice of ranking properties: stellar mass or star-forming rate (SFR). Stellar mass ranking top 20% halo are highly concentrate at low masses but suppressed by high masses; SFR ranking top 20% halo are gradually enhanced through mass growth.
  2. GAB signals are in principal higher in SFR ranking sample than stellar mass ranking sample. 
  3. Underlying responsible baryonic processes are evaluate by random forest algorithm: stellar-mass ranked sample are governed by gas cooling and stellar feedback; SFR ranked sample are dominant by star formation to gas cooling as number density increases.

### 4. Critical Commentary & Future Horizon
* **Strengths:** These process may possibly assign to observational data with proper SFR and stellar mass measured, I would be interest in what would be the result.
* **Limitations:** These results are limit to one redshift slice but not utilzing the evolutionary data from cosmological simulation. Why not directly check if these result conclude to the same by direct clustering process?
